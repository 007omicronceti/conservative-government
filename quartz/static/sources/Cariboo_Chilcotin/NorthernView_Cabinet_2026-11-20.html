B.C. Conservatives form government as new cabinet ministers are sworn in

Published 11:45 am Friday, November 20, 2026

By Mark Page/Black Press

The B.C. Conservative cabinet getting ready to get sworn in by Lt.-Gov. Wendy Cocchia in Victoria on Nov. 20, 2026. (Mark Page/Today In BC)
Getting your Trinity Audio player ready...

Lt.-Gov. Wendy Cocchia swore in Lorne Doerkson as British Columbia’s 38th premier on Friday morning.

Doerkson’s elevation to the premiership follows a stunning reversal of fortunes for the B.C. Conservative Party over the two months since Kerry-Lynne Findlay quit as leader, a behind-the-scenes deal was struck to elevate Doerkson to the role while he wasn’t even in the country, and former premier David Eby called a self-destructive snap election.

Doerkson addressed his wife, Shelley, and the circumstances in which they both found out he’d been named interim leader. They were in Amsterdam Schiphol Airport coming home from a trip “when we found out we were going to do this.”

“I remember you turning around and walking away from me in the Amsterdam airport,” he said. “I promise you, I didn’t know this either.”

This officially begins the tenure of B.C.’s first Conservative government this century.

Doerkson laid out his vision for governance with a message of unity.

“A mill in the north, a manufacturer in the Interior, a farmer in the Fraser Valley, a port on the coast. They might be hundreds of kilometres apart, but they are all part of the same economy and the same story. This is so important in this province to remember: what we produce in one community, creates jobs and opportunities in another and when one region succeeds, so the should the entire province.”

Along with the premier, Cocchia inaugurated the new cabinet ministers, with Peter Milobar taking on the finance role, Caroline Elliott serving as attorney general, and Ryan Peterson to be health minister.

Skeena MLA Claire Rattée has been named deputy premier.

Doerkson opted to shrink the number of ministries from 23 to 18.

Milobar’s appointment comes as no surprise. He had left the Conservative Party under former leader Kerry-Lynne Findlay, briefly leading the CentreBC party. After Findlay quit and Eby called the snap election, Milobar cut a deal to rejoin under Doerkson.

Elliott’s appointment was also expected, despite her being a freshman legislator. She is popular within the party and came within fewer than 100 votes of defeating Findlay in the party’s leadership race in May.

Other notable appointments include Delta South MLA Ian Paton for agriculture, Chilliwack-Cultus Lake MLA A’a:líya Warbus for environment, Prince George-Mackenzie MLA Kiel Giddens for forests, Columbia River-Revelstoke MLA Scott McInnis for Indigenous relations and reconciliation and Surrey-Serpentine River MLA for public safety and solicitor general.

However, longtime Richmond MLA Teresa Wat – who left the party under Findlay but returned with Doerkson – was notably absent from the cabinet.

Several Findlay loyalists were left out of cabinet positions including Chilliwack North MLA Heather Maahs, Prince George-North Cariboo MLA Sheldon Clare, Salmon Arm-Shuswap MLA David Williams and West Kelowna-Peachland MLA Macklin McCall.
