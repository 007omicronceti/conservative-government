      New B.C. government announces its 1st cabinet

      Lorne Doerkson announced his cabinet Friday after being sworn in as B.C.'s 38th premier by Lt.-Gov. Wendy Cocchia.

      Ceremony held at Government House in Victoria

      Lorne Doerkson was sworn in as B.C.'s 38th premier Friday, alongside his newly announced cabinet ministers.

      Lt.-Gov. Wendy Cocchia led the ceremony at Government House in Victoria.

      The government's new ministers include:

      • Premier: Lorne Doerkson
      • Minister of Agriculture: Ian Paton
      • Attorney General: Caroline Elliott
      • Minister of Children and Family Development: Kristina Loewen
      • Minister of Citizens' Services and Technology: Bruce Banman
      • Minister of Economic Development and Tourism: Gavin Dew
      • Minister of State for Red Tape Reduction: Carson Binda
      • Minister of State for Rural Development: Anna Kindy
      • Minister of Education and Child Care: Lynne Block
      • Minister of Energy, Mines and Petroleum Resources: Larry Neufeld
      • Minister of State for Mining: Pete Davis
      • Minister of Environment: Á'a:líya Warbus
      • Minister of Finance: Peter Milobar
      • Minister of Forests: Kiel Giddens
      • Minister of Health: Ryan Peterson
      • Minister of Indigenous Relations and Reconciliation: Scott McInnis
      • Minister of Local Government, Housing, Sport and Culture: Cathie Ounsted
      • Minister of State for Housing: Zeeshan Wahla
      • Minister of Post-Secondary Education and Labour: Korky Neufeld
      • Minister of State for Labour: Harman Bhangu
      • Minister of Public Safety and Solicitor General: Jessy Sahota
      • Minister of State for Emergency Management: Ward Stamer
      • Minister of Social Development and Deputy Premier: Claire Rattée
      • Minister of Transportation and Infrastructure: Trevor Halford
      • Minister of Water, Land and Natural Resource Operations: Donegal Wilson

      Parliamentary secretaries include:

      • Armed Forces and Veterans Affairs: Rosalyn Bird
      • Children and Youth with Special Needs: Margareta Dovgal
      • Insurance Corporation of British Columbia: Steve Kooner
      • Infrastructure: Misty Van Popta
      • Metro Vancouver: Megan Knight
      • Rural Health: Tracy St. Claire
      • Seniors: Sharon Hartwell
      • Sport and Culture: Mandeep Dhaliwal
      • Technology and Innovation: Sacha Peter
      • Tourism: Jody Toor
      • TransLink: Harj Dhaliwal
      • Urban Policing: Bryan Tepper

      Among the invited guests to the ceremony was former B.C. Conservative leader John Rustad.

      "The hardest job any premier does is to create a cabinet, because the majority of people that go into cabinet didn't necessarily get what they wanted, and the majority of people that didn't go into cabinet would have loved to have been there," he said.

      B.C. Lt.-Gov. Wendy Cocchia is seen at the legislature on Feb. 12, 2026. She will lead a swearing-in ceremony for the province's next premier, Lorne Doerkson, on Friday. (Chad Hipolito/The Canadian Press)

      It is the first time the B.C. Conservative Party has been elected to government in more than 100 years.

      The Conservatives' majority, the largest the province has seen in 25 years, comes in the wake of a snap election called by former NDP premier David Eby last month.

      When the NDP formed government in 2017, after a confidence agreement with the B.C. Greens, there were no Conservative MLAs in the legislature.

      • B.C. Conservative caucus sworn in ahead of government transition

      On Thursday, 63 B.C. Conservative MLAs were sworn in — with one to take their oath of office pending a judicial recount next week.

      Doerkson has said there will not be a fall legislative session, in order to give his new cabinet members time to familiarize themselves with their new files.
