* **Office:** [[01_Ministries/01_Office_of_the_Premier|Office of the Premier]] (sworn in November 20, 2026)

## 1. Early Life, Pre-Political Career & Regional Roots
Born in New Westminster, B.C., Lorne Doerkson moved with his family to Salmon Arm at age 13 and competed as a rodeo rider in his youth before settling in the Cariboo-Chilcotin nearly three decades ago, making his home in Williams Lake with his wife, Tracey, and their two children.

Before entering provincial politics, Doerkson spent **20 years in community newspaper publishing with Black Press**, serving as publisher of the *Williams Lake Tribune* and regional papers across the Cariboo. Through that role, he sat on the executive board of the **B.C. Community Newspaper Association**, giving him direct working relationships with municipal councils, regional district directors, forestry employers, and local advertisers across the central Interior. He subsequently worked as a financial consultant with **Investors Group** in Williams Lake.

His pre-political civic footprint in Williams Lake and the South Cariboo was extensive:
* **Williams Lake Stampede Association:** Former director and active organizer.
* **Williams Lake & District Chamber of Commerce:** Past executive and two-time recipient of the Chamber's **Community Booster Award**.
* **Hough Memorial Cancer Society:** Board member supporting local cancer diagnostic and treatment equipment fundraising in the Cariboo.

## 2. Electoral Record & Party Realignment (2020–2026)
Doerkson's rise to the Premier's Office reflects the collapse of the B.C. Liberal / BC United coalition and the consolidation of the Interior vote:
* **2020 Provincial Election:** Won Cariboo-Chilcotin for the B.C. Liberals with 51.25% of the vote, succeeding retiring Liberal MLA Donna Barnett and defeating NDP candidate Scott Elliott.
* **Opposition Roles (2020–2024):** Appointed Assistant Deputy Speaker of the Legislative Assembly and served as BC United critic for Water, Land and Resource Stewardship as well as Rural Development.
* **May 31, 2024 Floor-Crossing:** Left BC United to join John Rustad's Conservative Party of B.C.—a pivotal defection in the Interior months before BC United suspended its campaign.
* **2024 Provincial Election:** Re-elected in Cariboo-Chilcotin as a BC Conservative with nearly 70% of the vote, and subsequently served as Official Opposition Leader in the legislature.
* **September 2026 Leadership & Snap Election:** Following Kerry-Lynne Findlay's resignation amid caucus turmoil and the CentreBC split, Doerkson was named interim BC Conservative leader days before Premier David Eby called a snap election on September 22, 2026. Doerkson brokered a reunification agreement bringing [[02_Cabinet/Peter_Milobar|Peter Milobar]], [[02_Cabinet/Ian_Paton|Ian Paton]], [[02_Cabinet/Donegal_Wilson|Donegal Wilson]], [[02_Cabinet/Aaliya_Warbus|Á'a:líya Warbus]], and [[02_Cabinet/Bruce_Banman|Bruce Banman]] back into the fold, and led the party to a **63-seat majority government on October 24, 2026**.
* **Swearing-In (November 20, 2026):** Sworn in as B.C.'s 38th Premier—the first Premier from the B.C. Interior since Kelowna's Bill Bennett left office in 1986.

## 3. Caucus Dynamics & Cabinet Architecture
* **Reunification Power-Sharing:** Rewarded CentreBC returnees with top economic and resource portfolios—appointing [[02_Cabinet/Peter_Milobar|Peter Milobar]] as Minister of Finance, [[02_Cabinet/Donegal_Wilson|Donegal Wilson]] to Water, Land and Natural Resource Operations, [[02_Cabinet/Ian_Paton|Ian Paton]] to Agriculture, [[02_Cabinet/Aaliya_Warbus|Á'a:líya Warbus]] to Environment, and [[02_Cabinet/Bruce_Banman|Bruce Banman]] to Citizens' Services.
* **Findlay Loyalists Excluded:** Left prominent Kerry-Lynne Findlay allies out of cabinet—most notably neighboring Prince George–North Cariboo MLA **Sheldon Clare** (who represents Quesnel and parts of the North Cariboo), concentrating Cariboo cabinet influence almost entirely in Doerkson's own office.
* **Legislative Reset:** Cancelled the Fall 2026 legislative sitting to conduct cabinet onboarding and prepare spring 2027 legislation.

## 4. Cariboo-Chilcotin & 100 Mile House Accountability Watchlist
Because the Premier is also the local MLA for Cariboo-Chilcotin, every provincial policy test lands directly in his own backyard:
1. **100 Mile House ER & Cariboo Memorial Hospital:** Campaigned explicitly on ending rural emergency department diversions (28 closures / 377 hours at 100 Mile District General Hospital in 2025, continuing into 2026) and delivering the $367M Cariboo Memorial Hospital expansion in Williams Lake under his 100-day *Patients First* health review ([[04_Dossiers/Cariboo_Chilcotin/Dossier_Cariboo_Healthcare_CMH_100MileER|Healthcare Dossier]]).
2. **DRIPA Repeal & Aboriginal Title:** Pledged to repeal the *Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act* (DRIPA)—a move with immediate legal, forestry, and Crown-land consequences across **Tsilhqot'in**, **Secwépemc**, and **Ulkatcho** territories inside his own riding ([[04_Dossiers/Cariboo_Chilcotin/Dossier_DRIPA_Repeal_Cariboo_Title|DRIPA & Cariboo Title Dossier]]).
3. **Tolko & 100 Mile Timber Supply:** Appointed former Tolko Industries VP and registered lobbyist [[03_Deputy_Ministers/Janis_Simpkins|Janis Simpkins]] as Deputy Minister of Forests while promising to fast-track permitting and stabilize fibre supply after mill closures in 100 Mile House and the Cariboo ([[04_Dossiers/Cariboo_Chilcotin/Dossier_Tolko_Cariboo_Timber|Tolko Dossier]]).
4. **Yellowhead Copper & Cariboo Gold:** Oversees permitting acceleration affecting the [[04_Dossiers/Cariboo_Chilcotin/Dossier_Yellowhead_Copper_100Mile|Yellowhead Copper]] transmission tie-in to the 100 Mile House BC Hydro substation and labour/compliance disputes at the [[04_Dossiers/Cariboo_Chilcotin/Dossier_Cariboo_Gold_Project|Cariboo Gold Project]] in Wells.
